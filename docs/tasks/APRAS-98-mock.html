Focus indicator on the slug-change dialog

This task moves keyboard focus into the confirmation panel for the first time. Today its two buttons have no focus style at all, so a keyboard user would land on an invisible selection. Press Tab inside any panel below, or use the toggle, to see the ring.

--ring = oklch(0.62 0.15 160) ≈ #009f68 It is the brand emerald — the same value as --primary, not a blue. Against the white panel it measures 3.41:1, just over the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 floor for non-text indicators. Every ratio on this page comes from importing src/lib/contrast.ts, which gamut-maps rather than clipping the out-of-gamut --ring.

2. The open question: should the ring have a gap?

Both options below pass WCAG. The difference is whether the green ring touches the button or is separated from it by a thin white gap. It matters most on the red “Alterar mesmo assim” button: where green meets red directly, the two measure 1.41:1 against each other, so that edge of the ring all but disappears. A card-coloured gap gives the ring a 3.41:1 boundary on both sides.

The spec now picks B: the gap is the one that holds up on the red “Alterar mesmo assim” button, which is the irreversible action, and nothing in CI measures that 1.41:1 edge. B is also what the house button component src/components/ui/button.tsx uses, while A is what the sibling dialog src/components/ui/alert-modal.tsx hand-rolls. Either is defensible; A or B is a complete answer.

3. The same two options in dark mode

Not a separate question — the answer to § 2 applies to both themes. This panel exists because the numbers change: on the dark card --ring is oklch(0.65 0.15 160) ≈ #00aa6f and measures 6.42:1 against the card (#0a0f0c) — far clearer than light mode's 3.41:1 — while against the dark destructive fill (#d40924) it is still only 1.80:1. The weak edge that motivates B is weak in both themes.

The third panel is why the spec writes ring-offset-card rather than copying button.tsx as-is: a bare ring-offset-2 resolves to Tailwind v4's hard-coded #fff, painting a white seam around the button on a dark card.
