The acting condominium's logo in the header

Every state the spec defines, rendered on the real header strip. The logo slot sits in the header's left group, where today's generic APRAS dot lives. Nothing below is a real tenant asset — the sample logos are inline SVG stand-ins for a square emblem, a wide banner and a dark-ink mark.

1. The six states

Six states, five cards: “no logo configured”, “still loading” and “the profile read failed” all render the same thing, so they share one card rather than being drawn three times.

Question 1 — Does the logo show on a desktop screen, or only on the phone? Answered: A

On a phone the header already shows a little APRAS dot. On a desktop screen that dot is hidden, because the left menu already shows “APRAS”. Option A puts the condominium's logo in the header on every screen size; option B keeps it a phone-only thing. The operator chose A, which is what the spec recommended: on a desktop screen nothing at all tells you which condominium you are in when you belong to only one.

Question 2 — In the dark colour scheme, does a dark logo get a white card behind it? Answered: B

Many condominium logos are drawn in black ink for printing. On the dark scheme they nearly disappear. Option A shows the logo plainly, exactly as the condominium drew it. Option B puts a small white card behind it so it is always readable — at the cost of a white patch in an otherwise dark bar, for every logo, including the ones that did not need it. The operator chose B, so the card ships: in the dark scheme a logo sits on a small light card, and in the light scheme nothing paints behind it. Both options stay drawn below as the record of the choice; the dark-scheme card in section 1 now shows what was chosen.
